## The tip finding — **result, not bug**

The reference-aligned push tips a light **cube** roughly **90°** during
contact. Interpretation confirmed as task-geometry physics:

1. **The reference doesn't push cubes.** dairlib's `sampling_c3_public` runs
   on a low-CoM T-shape (`push_t`) or `jacktoy` — the wide base and low
   moment of inertia damp any pitch. The reference's mechanism was never
   validated against a 200 g cube.

2. **Cube tipping threshold is ~2 mm.** For a 100 mm cube pushed at ~44 N
   (the reference's u-bound magnitude), the tipping condition
   `(force · moment_arm) > (weight · half_base)` requires a moment arm of
   only ~2 mm — far below the pusher's 25 mm sphere radius. **Any reachable
   contact height off dead-centre trips it.**

3. **The climb amplifies tip to flip.** Pinned in §7.75c:
   - Tip crosses 0 → planner sees tilted face normal
   - Plan's `x_seq[1][9]` rises to keep contact on the linearized face
   - OSC drives sphere up (`Kp_z · (x_seq[1][9] − ee_z_now)`, no z-clamp)
   - Sphere rides higher on tilted face → larger moment arm → more tip
   - Positive feedback: partial lean → full 90° flip

4. **Loop-break candidates were designed** (see `§7.75c_repro/REPORT.md`):
   (i) planner QP state bound, (ii) cost-side z-hold penalty, (iii) OSC
   z-target clamp. **Not landed.** Analysis: (ii) would reduce the flip to
   a small lean at partial closure cost; (iii) fights the plan under
   W_track authority; (i) requires new ADMM state-bounds plumbing. The
   right answer is the reference's task-geometry choice — push a low-CoM
   object, not a cube. Loop-break is scope for a follow-up phase, not the
   alignment push.

5. **Deferred:** a reference/dairlib run on a cube (requires the Bazel/Drake
   toolchain build; §7.75 spike attempted, not completed) would confirm the
   tip is physics rather than port-specific. Not on the critical path for
   the C3-vs-C3+ study, but the definitive falsifier if ever wanted.
