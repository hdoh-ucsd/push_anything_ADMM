Does the GPU accelerate our ADMM?

No. On the real C3+ hot loop the batched GPU solver runs ten times slower than Drake's CPU OSQP. The reasons are structural, not implementation polish — and they were only visible once the benchmark used real matrices instead of random ones.

The reconciliation

A 4.4× per-iteration win, erased by 133× more iterations

Per iteration, the GPU really is faster. The problem is how many iterations it needs. Drake's OSQP converges to eps = 1e-5 in 100–200 iterations. Our batched solver ran 20,000 and still had not converged — primal residual stuck at 2.1e-4.

Our solver implements the plain OSQP iteration. I had scoped Ruiz equilibration and adaptive ρ out of the plan as fidelity details — things you'd need to match OSQP's exact path, not its answer. That was wrong. On a problem with cond(K) ≈ 1.2e7, preconditioning is the performance; without it a first-order method crawls.

Structural reason 1

The Hessian is diagonal

This is the fact that decides the question. The C3+ cost Hessian is not “sparse-ish” — at 659 nonzeros in 639×639 it is 0.16% dense, essentially a diagonal. A dense batched GPU solve does O(n²) and O(n³) work on it. Drake's sparse factorization does not.

scripts/gpu/sparsity_probe.py
Structural reason 2

This GPU has no fp64 headroom

GeForce Blackwell runs float64 at 1/64 the float32 rate. The RTX 5070 Ti delivers roughly 0.7 TFLOPS fp64 — about par with the 7800X3D's theoretical peak. So there is no raw throughput surplus to pay for throwing away 11× sparsity. The conformance mission rules out dropping to fp32, where the card would be 64× faster.

Where my first number came from

The benchmark that misled me

An earlier measurement showed the GPU winning 4.41× on a full OSQP iteration, and it was that number that opened the gate to build the solver. It was measured against the wrong opponent: dense linear algebra on the CPU, not the sparse C++ solver the port actually runs.

Why cuNRTO's 25.9× doesn't carry over

The paper's Franka speedup is real, and its architecture is sound — we adopted it faithfully. It does not transfer because their subproblem is a different animal: thousands of second-order-cone constraints and a dense feedback-gain block of 2,940 variables. Ours is n = 639 at 0.16% Hessian density. GPUs win on large and dense; this problem is small and sparse. The paper also benchmarks against MOSEK interior-point at 40–80 ADMM iterations, where we benchmark against OSQP — same algorithm class as ours, at 3.

Not a wasted arc

What the investigation did produce

  • A repaired parallel sample-eval path Three independent defects. Worker plant contexts were built standalone, so geometry_query was unconnected and every worker LCS build threw — swallowed by a bare except, producing NaN costs. Workers also ran a different physics model (4 unforwarded constructor args, including controller_inertia). threads=4 went from total task failure to tight_goal=PASS(final).
  • A hard answer on threading Drake's Solve does not release the GIL — 0.99× on 4 threads, against a 3.99× sleep control. The reference's OpenMP parallel-for cannot translate to Python threads at any thread count. That closes a lever your notes had listed as the biggest remaining speedup.
  • A reusable solver-level test corpus 60 real ADMM instances with CPU golden outputs and the assembled QP matrices — the substrate any future solver change can be validated against without a sim run.
  • A measured record of what the port's hot loop actually is Diagonal Hessian, 9% dense KKT, cond(K) ≈ 1.2e7, 100–200 OSQP iterations, 150 × 6 × 3 solves per tick. Any future performance work starts from these numbers instead of guesses.
Recommendation

Stop the GPU port; the profile points elsewhere

Nothing about this hardware or this problem size rewards a batched dense GPU solver. I'd stop at the current commit rather than build the preconditioner for a projected 3%.

If solve time is still the target, the honest next moves are ordered by the profile, not by hardware appeal: cut the number of solves (6 samples × 3 iterations × ~150 OSQP iterations per tick is the real budget), or cut the cost per solve by exploiting the diagonal Hessian directly — the structure the current dense formulation discards. Both act on the 85% of wall time that is ADMM sample evaluation. A GPU would become the right tool if the horizon or contact count grew by an order of magnitude; at n = 639 it is not.
